# Double Down Madness: findings

## Verdict

**Version 1 Double Down Madness is countable and beatable when it is dealt from a
deep six-deck shoe. Hi-Lo is already a very good betting count.** Its exact
betting correlation is 0.9779; the simplest count derived from this game's own
effects of removal is exactly Hi-Lo. A much harder level-6 count improves BC to
0.9981, but only reduced sampled N0 by about 5% before strategy indices.

The practical caveat is larger than the mathematical result: a continuous
shuffler has no pre-deal count variation, so it provides no betting opportunity.
The 100-million-round CSM control remained a house game at **0.8754% ± 0.0327%**,
consistent with the exact 0.8860% chart edge. A shoe must also be deep enough.
With the tested 1–3–7–11–15–16 ramp, break-even is around two decks dealt, but
that boundary has an unusably large N0. The attractive result requires much
deeper penetration.

This is a research result, not gambling advice. Casino availability, limits,
procedures, game speed and tolerance of a 1–16 spread are not modelled.

## Audited rules and validation

Primary rule sources are [Wizard of Odds](https://wizardofodds.com/games/blackjack/double-down-madness/)
and [Canterbury Park's posted rules](https://www.canterburypark.com/blackjack/double-down-madness/).
The model uses six decks, H17, peek, strict first-card-ace restriction, unlimited
re-doubling, dealer 22 pushes, and Version 1's suit-blended 1.625 blackjack
payout. See `HANDOFF.md` for the full rule audit and modelling details.

`python validate_wizard.py --full --rounds 1000000000` passed all 12 checks:

| Check | Target | Result |
|---|---:|---:|
| P(dealer draws to 22), 6d H17 | 0.073536 | **0.073536** |
| Push 22 house edge at 11:1 | 11.76% | **11.7565%** |
| V2 and V3 exact edge difference | 0 | **0** |
| Derived chart vs Wizard chart | approximately equal | **0.0033% better** |
| CSM MC vs exact chart edge | 0.8860% | **0.8799%** |
| V1 six-deck cut-card edge | 0.95% | **0.9442%** |
| Average final wager | 1.57 | **1.5877** |
| Element of risk | 0.61% | **0.5947%** |
| Cut-card cost | 0.03% | **0.0643%** |

The last row remains an explained discrepancy rather than an exact match:
Wizard publishes a difference rounded to two decimals of a percent. The engine
also measures the eight-deck penalty as 0.052% versus his rounded 0.03%. Both
directions agree and neither affects beatability.

## What the exact engine established

1. **Dealer 22 is the game.** Removing the push turns a 0.8853% house edge into
   a 10.1977% player edge. The rule is worth about 11.1 percentage points.
2. **One-card hands need their own chart.** A lone ten can double into a paid
   blackjack; a multi-card hard 10 cannot. Collapsing those states costs about
   0.37% and creates an apparent contradiction in Wizard's strategy prose.
3. **The ace-rule dispute does not affect Version 1.** Strict and `double_only`
   interpretations both produce a 0.8853% fresh-shoe house edge because doubling
   a lone ace is optimal against every upcard in Version 1.
4. **Soft 12 is unreachable under the strict rule.** A first-card ace receives
   exactly one additional card, so A+A never reaches another decision.
5. **The derived chart is 0.0033% better than Wizard's chart.** The four
   differing cells are marginal doubles and have no practical significance.

ramp. That gain is real but modest for the added
mental load. The recommended small-integer count is therefore **Hi-Lo**.

## Strategy indices

Indices were generated by exact chart extraction at 17 fixed-size, four-deck
compositions representing Hi-Lo TC −8 through +8. Whole-round exact
re-evaluation then scored each one-cell change at its boundary composition.
There were 91 clean flips and two ambiguous cells. Locally selected flips with
negative whole-round value were excluded from simulation.

The simulation uses the top 18 positive-gain play indices plus **insurance at
TC +4**. The largest boundary gains include hard 14 vs 3 hit at TC ≤−5, hard 12
vs 6 hit at TC ≤−3, hard 12 vs 5 hit at TC ≤−1, hard 16 vs 10 stand at TC ≥+7,
and first-card 8 vs 5 hit at TC ≤−2. The complete audited list, including gains
and ambiguous cells, is in `results/indices_v1_hilo.json`.

On the same 1–3–7–11–15–16 ramp over one billion rounds:

| Policy | EV/round | N0 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Basic strategy | +0.070085 ± 0.000519 | 14,297 |
| + insurance TC +4 | +0.072659 ± 0.000519 | 13,270 |
| + top 18 play indices | **+0.073899 ± 0.000521** | **12,923** |

Insurance contributes most of the index gain. Exact thresholds are still a
model of a one-parameter count: unusual real compositions with the same TC can
have different optimal actions.

An independent fixed-ramp confirmation using seed `20260826` and
**9,999,999,996 rounds** produced **+0.073979 ± 0.000165 units/round**, SD
**8.4010**, and N0 **12,896**. The EV differs from the original one-billion
result by only +0.000081 units/round, well inside its confidence interval. The
CountLab long-run calculator uses this tighter ten-billion-round result and its
17 true-count buckets; the artifact is `results/main_hilo_indices_10b.json`.

## Ramp, bankroll and hourly result

Main benchmark: Version 1, six decks, one deck cut off, one occupied table spot,
exact deck estimation, Hi-Lo, insurance + top 18 indices, one billion rounds.
All confidence intervals below are sampled 95% intervals.

| Ramp | EV/round | SD/round | N0 | Bankroll for 5% lifetime RoR per $1 unit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| OP 1–2–4–8–16 | +0.066131 ± 0.000476 | 7.6734 | 13,464 | 1,334 units |
| Optimized 1–3–7–11–15–16 | **+0.073899 ± 0.000521** | 8.4006 | **12,923** | 1,430 units |

The optimized ramp improves SCORE/N0 by about 4%. At an assumed 100 rounds per
hour it earns **7.390 base units/hour** with **84.006 units/hour SD**. A $10
minimum scales those to $73.90/hour, $840.06/hour SD, and about **$14,304** for
5% lifetime risk of ruin. With a $10,000 bankroll and the same 5% risk target,
the risk-sized base unit is **$6.99** (about $51.66/hour expected value).

These hourly figures scale linearly with actual game speed. Multi-player rows in
the sweep mean total occupied table spots, not multiple simultaneous hands owned
by the counter. `ddm.bankroll` includes CountLab's 0.3724 simultaneous-hand
correlation convention, but this report does not claim a multi-hand result.

## Answers to the original questions

**Were the rules modelled correctly?** The important rules are now explicit and
source-backed. The original project's statement that Wizard recommends hitting
an ace vs ace in Version 1 was a source misread; that advice applies to Versions
2 and 3. One-card hands must also be modelled separately.

**Was dealer-22 logic right?** Treating exactly 22 as a push and 23+ as an
ordinary bust is correct. Exact dealer recursion reproduces Wizard's six-digit
22 probability. Removing that rule changes player EV by about 11.1 percentage
points, so even a small settlement error here overwhelms the count result.

**What simulation mistakes mattered?** Assuming Hi-Lo before computing EORs was
methodologically weak but happened to work. More important hazards were using
the pre-deal count for play deviations, omitting insurance settlement, treating
one-card 10 as multi-card hard 10, reversing the EOR/tag correlation sign, and
silently limiting penetration with an oversized round reserve. Each now has an
exact check, deterministic regression test, or explicit exhaustion failure.

**What makes the result trustworthy?** Exact combinatorial results establish
the rules, base strategy, EORs and index boundary values. Seeded Monte Carlo is
used only for cut-card, count-frequency, variance and ramp questions. It is
cross-validated against the exact CSM result, sampled outputs carry 95%
confidence intervals, emitted artifacts record their rule configuration, and
the deviation path has a deterministic equivalence test.
